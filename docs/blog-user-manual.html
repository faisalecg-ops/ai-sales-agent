1. Signing in, and who can do what

Each exhibition's blog has its own admin at /blog/admin on that exhibition's website — for example https://buildasia.net/blog/admin. Sign in with your email and password. After five wrong passwords, sign-in pauses for 15 minutes for safety.

The Dashboard shows what needs attention: articles waiting for approval, your drafts, comments to approve, broken links, the most read articles and — for admins — a Finish setting up list.

RoleCan do
AuthorWrites their own articles and sends them for approval. Uploads images.
EditorEdits any article, runs the home page, slider, sponsor banners, categories, link library and comments. Sends articles for approval.
ApproverEverything an editor does, plus approves, publishes, schedules and unpublishes articles.
AdminEverything, including branding, settings, users, subscribers and API keys.

Change your own name, photo, short biography and password under your name at the top right (Your profile).

2. Writing an article

Go to Articles → + New article.

  1. Title — clear and specific. The web address is made from it automatically.
  2. Standfirst — one or two sentences under the title. It also appears on the article cards and in Google.
  3. The text — type or paste. Pasting from Word or Google Docs keeps headings, lists, bold and links, and drops the rest of their formatting. The toolbar gives:
    • Paragraph ▾ — turn a line into a Heading or Sub-heading. Use headings to break up long articles; three or more make an automatic “In this article” list.
    • B I U, bullet and numbered lists, ❝ Quote.
    • 🔗 Link — select words first, then add the address. See section 9.
    • 🖼 Image — pick from the media library or upload; you can add a caption.
    • ▶ Video — paste a YouTube or Vimeo address.
    • ▦ Table, ― divider, undo/redo, and </> to see the HTML code.
  4. Category and Tags on the right. Every article needs a category; tags are optional (separate with commas).
  5. Featured image — shown at the top of the article and on cards. Use a wide photo, at least 1600 pixels across.
  6. Google and social media — the box shows how the article will appear in Google. You can leave the SEO title and description empty: the title and standfirst are used.

Press Save draft at any time. When it is ready, press Send for approval. Use Preview to see exactly how it will look.

Tone. The blog is about the industry's development: market insight, technology, practical advice, success stories. Mention the exhibition where it naturally helps the reader — the “Courtesy of” box under every article already invites them to visit and exhibit.

Version history at the bottom keeps the last 50 versions. Restore brings back an earlier text; the current one stays in the history.

3. Approving and publishing

Articles waiting for approval appear on the dashboard and at the top of Articles. Open one, read it, press Check links, then on the right:

ButtonWhat happens
Approve & publish nowIt goes live on the blog at once. The sitemap updates, and the Social Media Agent is told (when connected).
Schedule for laterChoose a date and time (Pakistan time). It publishes by itself.
Send back to the writerReturns it as a draft with your note at the top, explaining what to change.
UnpublishTakes a live article off the blog and makes it a draft again.
ArchiveRemoves it from the blog and from the normal list (find it with the “Everything” filter).

Editing a published article with Save changes (live) updates it on the blog straight away. If you change its web address, the old address keeps working and forwards readers and Google to the new one.

4. Articles from the Social Media Agent

When the Social Media Agent is set up (the next phase), its articles arrive in Articles marked Agent and awaiting approval. At the top of the article you will see which sources the agent says it used. Check the facts, the tone and every link, then approve, schedule or send back as usual. Nothing the agent writes appears on the blog until a person approves it, unless an admin has deliberately given its key permission to publish.

5. Images

Media library: drag images onto the page or press choose files. JPEG, PNG, WebP or GIF up to 12 MB. Each image is resized automatically so phones download a small version and big screens a sharp one.

Click an image to add a description (for blind readers and Google — say what the picture shows), a caption and a photo credit. Images without a description are marked add description. An image that is still used somewhere cannot be deleted.

Only use photos you have the right to use: your own show photos, photos supplied by exhibitors for this purpose, or licensed images. Never copy images from news sites.

6. The home page and the slider

Home page builder: tick the sections to show and drag them into order (or use the arrows). Changes are live as soon as you save.

  • Featured articles: in the article editor, tick Show in “Featured”. The lowest Featured order number becomes the large story.
  • Slider: each slide has a picture, headline, short text, a button and optional show from / stop after dates — a slide promoting early registration can switch itself off when registration closes. Choose fade or slide-sideways and the seconds per slide under Slider settings.
  • Event panel: the countdown and buttons come from Event & courtesy (section 8).
  • Counter strip: up to six figures (for example 32000 + Trade visitors, 190000 + sq ft Exhibition space) that count up when readers scroll to them, as on the main website. Fill them in at the bottom of the Home page builder; use the latest post-show report figures. With no figures the strip is hidden.

7. Sponsor banners and reports

Sponsor banners → + New banner: the sponsor's name, the banner image (and an optional narrower phone version), the link, where it appears, and optional start and end dates. Banners stop by themselves after their end date. When several banners share a place, they take turns.

PlaceImage size
Top or middle of the home page1200 × 200 px
Above the footer on every page1200 × 150 px
Inside articles, after the text760 × 160 px
Sidebar next to articles300 × 250 px or 300 × 600 px

Sponsor report (bottom of the page): choose the sponsor and the dates, press Open report. A one-page report opens, branded with the exhibition's logo, showing how often the banners were shown, the clicks and the click rate, day by day. Press Print / Save as PDF to send it to the sponsor, or Spreadsheet for the figures. Search-engine robots are never counted.

8. Event details and the “Courtesy of” box

The exhibition's name, dates, venue and the three website addresses (main website, register to visit, book a stand) feed the countdown, the buttons, the “Courtesy of” box and Google's event listing.

When the sales agent is connected (section 12), these come from the sales agent's own Dates & venue settings automatically, once a day. A box left empty shows the sales agent's value in grey; anything you type in the blog takes priority.

Here you also choose the wording and placement of the links back to the main website: the header button (“Visit Build Asia ↗”, folded into the menu on phones), the “Courtesy of” box under every article, and the footer credit. Write {event} to insert the exhibition name. Contact details and social media pages appear in the footer.

9. Links in articles and the link library

Good articles link naturally on words in the text — usually 3 to 6 links. Allowed links are:

  • addresses in the link library (register, book a stand, exhibitor list, conference, sponsorship, venue, key articles);
  • other articles of the same blog;
  • sources actually used for the article.

In the article editor, Check links lists every link with a coloured label: green for library, this blog or a declared source; amber or red for anything else, to check before approving. It also suggests library links and related articles: select the words, then click the suggestion.

Done automatically: outside links open in a new tab; sponsor links are marked “sponsored” for Google; tracking tags (UTM) are removed from links inside articles. Every week the blog checks all links and lists broken ones under Link library → Broken-link check.

10. Branding and menus

Complete looks — one click sets colours, fonts and style together, shown live in the preview: Build Asia (navy and amber, Archivo, stripe — like buildasia.net), Corporate (navy and gold, clean font, boxed cards), Editorial (red and black, newspaper serif), Medical / Pharma (teal and orange, friendly font, soft shadows — for Pharma Asia or Health Asia) and Modern minimal (black and white). Adjust anything afterwards, then save.

  • Logos: main logo (for light backgrounds), a white version (for the dark header and footer), the browser tab icon, and a default picture for social media shares.
  • Colours: the blog starts in Build Asia's own colours (navy, royal blue and amber). Start from another preset or pick each colour. The highlight colour is used for the call-to-action buttons (Register to visit, Visit website), the slanted stripe and the counter labels. A readability check warns when text would be hard to read.
  • Fonts and style: headline and text fonts from a professional list, including Archivo, the main website's font (all stored on your own website, so pages stay fast); header style, card style, button shape, corner roundness and slider darkening.
  • Stripe: switch the slanted stripe under the header and above the footer on or off.
  • Motion: Full (sections rise in, figures count up, the stripe draws across, panels drift — like the website), Gentle (a soft fade only) or Off. Animations are always off for visitors whose device asks for less motion.
  • ↶ Undo last change restores the previous look in one click.

Menus: the header menu (usually the categories — use Quick add) and the footer menu (main-site pages such as the exhibitor list, conference and sponsorship).

11. Comments and the newsletter

Both are switched on or off in Readers & general.

Comments (off at first): when on, readers can comment and each comment waits in Comments until someone approves it. Spam robots are blocked automatically. Approved comments can be hidden again at any time.

Newsletter sign-up: when on, readers enter their email and must tick a consent box; they are added only after clicking the link in a confirmation email. The exact consent wording and the page are recorded. Subscribers (admins only) lists them and downloads the confirmed list with each person's unsubscribe link, which every newsletter must include.

12. The sales agent chat

Sales agent chat: tick Show the sales agent chat on every blog page, enter the exhibition's agent name (e.g. buildasia) and press Save and test connection. The chat appears on every blog page — home, articles, categories, search and the not-found page — loading after the page so it never slows it down. The same connection brings the event facts in section 8.

13. Users, Google, email, API keys and starter content

  • Users: add people, choose their role, switch accounts off. The blog always keeps at least one active admin. Untick Show this person's name on articles for staff accounts: their articles then show the exhibition as author.
  • SEO & Google: the home page's title and description, the Google Search Console code, and Hide the blog from Google (for a review copy only). The page also shows the sitemap address and the one line to add to the main website's robots.txt.
  • Email: the sender for newsletter confirmations, e.g. “Build Asia Secretariat” <info@buildasia.net>, ideally through the exhibition's own mail server. Save and send me a test checks it.
  • API keys (for the Social Media Agent, next phase): create a key and copy it — it is shown only once. Keep the permission on Drafts only so a person approves everything. The “Article published” notice address is where the blog tells the agent about new articles.
  • Activity log: who did what and when.
  • Starter content (admins): fills a new blog in one click. The Build Asia pack adds eleven categories (Market Insights, Construction Machinery, Building Materials, Housing & Real Estate, Sustainable Building, Exhibition News, Infrastructure & Projects, Cities & Urban Development, Industry & Manufacturing, Trade & Supply Chains, Economy & Business Climate) and 46 researched articles with cover images and their sources — plus, only where still empty, the event details, logo, menus, counter figures, slider, link library and contact details. Choose to publish them at once or add them as “awaiting approval” to review first. Nothing you have already set is changed, and importing twice does not duplicate anything. When a newer version of the pack arrives with the blog update, importing again adds only the new categories and articles. Starter articles are edited, unpublished or deleted like any other.
  • Readers & general: the blog's address, time zone, language, articles per page, and the scheduled-task status.

14. Questions

I changed something but the blog still shows the old version.

Pages are saved for speed and cleared automatically when you save in the admin. Reload the page in your browser. If it persists, Readers & general → Clear saved pages now.

A scheduled article did not appear on time.

Check Readers & general → Scheduled tasks shows “Running”. Without the cron job, scheduled articles still go out at the next visit to the blog.

How do I add a new category?

Categories & tags → fill in the form on the right. Add it to the header menu in Menus.

Can I delete an article?

Admins can delete articles that are not published. For a published article, Unpublish or Archive it instead — that keeps its history.

Can the blog be in Urdu?

English for now. The blog is built so other languages can be added later as a language file, including right-to-left Urdu.
